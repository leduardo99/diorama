# Style: Soulkeeper

Theme first: `src/theme.ts` is the source of truth. This file summarizes it and holds what the theme cannot express.

## Summary of the theme

- **Palette:** dusk purples for cloth (`robe`), crimson for leather and sashes (`sash`), ivory bone (`bone`), pale grey-violet skin (`flesh`), dark wood (`wood`), blue-grey iron (`iron`), stone (`stone`), and a mint-green soul fire (`soul`) as the only saturated accent.
- **Metals for rank (the ossuary captain):** `VOLUMES.steel` is polished steel, a step lighter than `iron` and darker than `blade`, so the captain's helm, greaves and bracers separate from the bone under them; `VOLUMES.brass` (`PALETTES.brass*`) is a muted gilt for fittings (pauldrons, the sword's guard and pommel, the crest's comb), far from the soul fire's saturation, which stays the only saturated accent. A gilded piece wears brass over the outfit's steel through its own `materials` override (`metal: 'brass'`), never a second set of parts.
- **Strokes:** pieces drawn with `@core/draw` alone (interface, effects) use `outline` (ribbon, 2.2) around silhouettes, `detail` (ribbon, 1.1) inside, `soulTrail` for wisps. Volume assets use `CONTOUR` (ink, 1.25 outside every object, set on `CAMERA`) and inner lines in a darker shade of each material.
- **Light:** volume assets (props, rig characters) are lit by `CAMERA` from the upper left. `soul` glows.
- **Space profile** (`src/space.ts`, `SPACE`): the game's space is declared once with `defineSpace` (the `jrpg` preset, character 80) and everything in the scenery reads it. Cells are 48 × 36 px on screen (a character is about 1.8 cells tall and under half a cell wide), a full wall is 100 (1.25 of a character), a door 92, a fence 44 (0.55), and walls and fences stand on cell BORDERS: the vertices where four cells meet, so a scene's `walls` grid is `(rows + 1) × (cols + 1)` and a room's floor ends exactly under its walls. Sizes come from `SPACE` (`import { SPACE } from '../space'`), never typed into a piece or a scene; change a proportion there. `scenery-test` and the scene asset (`figures: true`) draw a translucent mannequin of the character's height: judge every piece and every set next to one, and read `npm run inspect`'s 'off scale' warning (a sheet whose typical height is under 0.85 or over 1.4 of its nominal one).
- **Scenery kit** (`src/scenery`, catalog `SCENERY`): floors (grave dirt, crypt flagstones, dead moss, bone gravel, murky water) are calmer than the actors on them: one flat base tone, small soft detail, a thin dark line only where a material ends, and a value range narrower than a character's (flagstones keep the full range; dirt, moss, gravel and water stay under it on purpose). A frame is drawn the same wherever it is laid, so a field floor gets its look from variants (dirt, moss and gravel 5, flagstones and water 6) and a run of wall or fence from variants too (crypt wall 4, ruined wall 4, iron fence 4, wooden fence 3, appended so an index never changes its look), with large features soft and low in contrast and every border the same in every variant, so a field of one floor does not print a grid (flagstones keep their joints by design). Details keep their size in pixels next to the 80-unit character (a pebble, a blade, a ripple) and their count follows the tile's area (`per` in `floors.ts`, tuned on a 32 × 24 cell), so the 48 × 36 tiles are as finely grained as the old ones. Walls and fences use the same `CAMERA` and light as everything else, stone with mortar-coloured joint lines instead of stipple, one soul-green accent each (the ward runes burned into piers and posts, the gate's lantern). A wall or fence builds to the height its cell asks for (`ctx.height`: the crypt wall `wall` 100 or `fence` 44, the iron fence `fence` or `wall`, the wooden fence `fence`, the ruined wall `wall`): blocks, bars and posts keep their size, a taller piece has more courses or rails, never stretched ones. Kit materials are `VOLUMES.dirt`, `flagstone`, `mortar`, `moss`, `gravel` and `timber` (weathered grey timber, lighter than the dark `wood` so fences read against dirt); the catalog maps them to roles (`dirt`, `flagstone`, `stone`, `mortar`, `moss`, `gravel`, `metal`, `wood`, `bone`, `glow`).
- **Props and decals of the kit** (`scenery/props.ts`, `tree.ts`, `decals.ts`; one piece each in the catalog): tombstone, reliquary, coffin, candle cluster, bone pile, soul brazier, lantern post, dead tree; floor cracks, scattered bones, soul puddle. Props are volumes with the same ink contour and three-tone materials as the characters; the soul green stays the only saturated accent (flames, eyes, runes, the pool of light under a fire). Every prop is drawn at its design size and scaled so its top stands at its nominal height against the character (`PROP_HEIGHT` in `prop-parts.ts`: a tombstone 0.63, the reliquary 0.53, the brazier 0.5, a candle cluster 0.3, a bone pile 0.19, a lantern post 1.42, the dead tree 2.5 characters; the coffin about 1.05 characters long), and the catalog's `height` is that top. Every prop draws its contact shadow (and, for fires, a faint pool of soul light) into `ctx.ground`, which the engine paints under the parts and their contour, so the shadow never crosses the ink line; the parts go into `ctx.object`. A footprint turns with the facing (`footprintOf`): the coffin (1 × 2) covers 2 × 1 cells facing east or west, and the shadow follows; everything else takes one cell, the dead tree 2 × 2. Decals are flat and calm (thin lines, small detail) and fill their 48 × 36 tile (the exported cell is a little larger, 56 × 42). The four sets (`scenes/graveyard-corner.ts` 10 × 8, `crypt-entrance.ts` 9 × 7, `chapel-ruins.ts` 9 × 7, `flooded-crypt.ts` 10 × 8) are the reference compositions: a path or an aisle, clusters, a focal light, open ground. Their walls and fences stand on the cell borders and a room's floor ends exactly under them; a gap in a run is an empty vertex (one leaves about 76 clear between the end piers, two about 124: the crypt entrance's aisle and the flooded crypt's front doorway are the second), a doorway that closes is a door on an edge instead (see the living sets below), and a front the camera looks through is either open (the crypt entrance, the chapel, the graveyard) or closed with a LOW wall (the crypt wall at the fence's 44, as in the flooded crypt) so the room stays visible over it. Nothing but the lowest floor touches the open edges of a set (the graveyard's path at its gate is the one exception). Wall and fence looks that matter (a damaged block, a rune, a broken top) are chosen by hand per vertex (`SceneCell`), not left to the per-position pick, and a long straight run mixes whole, plain and damaged looks so it never repeats the same broken block or bent bar at an even step; a pier or post draws half an arm each way, so a look shows on both halves. The graveyard's back fence gives out into rotten timber at its right end (the wooden fence joins the iron one at the same height) and a small bed of bone gravel lies by the bone pile; the chapel's back wall is whole on the left and ruined on the right, and where its right wall is gone the dirt has come into the nave; the flooded crypt's pool lies over its flagstones, a coffin in the shallows.
- **Doors** (`scenery/doors.ts`, the iron gate in `fences.ts`; guide 05): they stand on a cell EDGE (`{ piece, col, row, edge: 'N' | 'W', state }`), cut the wall run (the walls end in piers, their jambs) and face across it, and have states and clips: the crypt door and the iron gate rest closed or open and play `opening` and `closing` (named so because `scenery-items` writes state and clip sheets into one `<id>--<name>` namespace, and `open` is a state), the crypt archway only rests open. Every frame is drawn from one pose read off the clip's key tables in frames (the leaf's angle, the ward's glow or the lantern's flare), so a clip starts on its `from` still and ends on its `to` one pixel for pixel; the leaf hangs on the jamb at the left hand of someone in the doorway looking out (the "observer" of the door's code and of its notes: the east jamb of a door facing S) and swings towards the way the door faces, up to square (anticipation of a few degrees, three frames of strike past square, a rebound and a settle; closing lifts, falls and slams with a rebound). The ward and the lantern are the soul accent. Details in the reference rows below.
- **Living props and navigation** (`scenery/interactables.ts`: the lever and the urn; the chest in `props.ts`; the shared timing and frame helpers in `motion.ts`; guide 05): a prop rests in `states` and plays `clips` between them, and every sheet's atlas says what it is in play (`nav`). Everything is drawn from the frame's pose (`ctx.state`, or `ctx.clip`), so a transition's first frame is exactly its `from` still and its last exactly its `to` still (checked pixel for pixel on every sheet), and a loop's frame 0 is the prop's still look (every wave in a loop is `wave(t, cycles, phase)`, zero at `t = 0`, and no loop uses `t = 1`). Transitions are hand-timed in frames (`track` on key tables: anticipation, a fast stroke over about three frames, overshoot and settle, the `events` on the frame the thing happens); clips are named for the action (`opening`, `closing`, `pull`, `push`, `break`), never for the state they end in, because a clip and a state share the name of their sheet (`<id>--<name>`). The chest rests `shut` or `open` (lid on its back hinge, candle snuffed, a soul orb on crossed bones in a wine-red lining) and plays `opening` (10 frames, lid at 118° on frame 5: `open`, a burst of soul light with motes) and `closing` (8 frames, slam on frame 5: `close`); the lever rests `up` or `down` and plays `pull` and `push` (8 frames, the arm meets its stop on frame 5: `click`, the gems in the hubs flash); the urn rests `whole` or `broken` and plays `break` (10 frames: it wobbles and cracks with soul light through the cracks on frames 1 and 2, bursts on frame 3: `break`, its shards fly out on arcs and settle by frame 9, ash and a wisp of soul rise and are gone). Loops: `soul-brazier` `burn` (8 frames at 10 fps), `candle-cluster` `flicker` (6 at 8), `lantern-post` `flicker` (6 at 8), `soul-puddle` `shimmer` (8 at 6); a clip sheet exports variant 0 and every facing. `nav` is true to the art: props block their (turned) footprint, so the tombstone, reliquary, candle cluster, brazier, lantern post, coffin, lever and dead tree (2 × 2) block, and the urn blocks until it is `broken` (`passableIn`); the bone pile is knee high and flat (0.19 of a character) and does not block (`blocks: false`), decals never block; floors cost 1, murky water 3 (a shallow flood to wade through, not deep water), and the chest, the lever and the urn list themselves as interactables (`chest`, `lever`, `break`). Ceramic is a kit material (`VOLUMES.ceramic`, role `ceramic`: a dusk violet-grey glaze) for the urn.
- **Living sets and navigation** (`scenes/_*.ts`, exported with `scenes/`; guide 05): the four sets use the living kit, and every scene atlas carries its `navigation`. Graveyard corner: the iron gate, shut, on the north edge of cell (5, 0) between the fence's posts (the fence runs end in posts there, the path's left lane is closed by fence), a whole urn by the right-hand tombstone and a smashed one by the cross at the front. Crypt entrance: the back wall runs right across and a shut crypt door stands on the edge of cell (4, 0), the lantern posts flank it one cell either side so the cell in front of the door stays free, the lever (up) is by the right lantern and the reliquary, as a shut chest, by the left one. Flooded crypt: the back door open on the next chamber (cell (4, 0), half a cell off the room's middle, which the lantern on the right balances), a whole urn by the lantern and a smashed one on the left. Chapel ruins: a shut vestry door in the whole part of the back wall (cell (2, 1), the yard behind it), a whole urn by the altar and a smashed one in the nave. A scene gives a door or a prop its state like any other placement (`state: 'closed'`); a game recomputes the navigation with `sceneNavigation(scene, SCENERY, { open: { [index]: true } })`. Checked by walking each atlas's own `navigation` (`findPath`, `canStep`, `moveIn`): every free cell of every set is reachable from its open front; the door's cell is reached in 6 to 11 steps (the flooded crypt's walk goes round the pool in 11 steps at cost 15, where wading straight up the middle is 7 steps at cost 17); a shut door blocks its edge and an open one frees it (in the chapel, 12 steps round the ruin shut, 8 through the door open); every interactable can be used from a cell that can be reached. Where the picture and the navigation differ: a lone pier (the crypt entrance's two pillars, the chapel's stump) stands in the picture and blocks nothing, because walls block along edges; the navigation is by cell, so a door's opening is the whole 48-wide cell (its jambs, 10 each side, do not stop `moveIn`); ruin breaches (38 high) and the low front wall (44) block like whole walls; the bone pile (flat, 15 high) and a smashed urn are walked over; the water costs 3 a cell wherever it looks deep.
- **2.5D:** `CAMERA` (tilt 48°, height boost 1.2, light azimuth 140° / elevation 45°, contour) and `VOLUMES.*` materials with form shading for volume assets.

## Specifications the theme cannot express

- **Resolution and export scale:** logical sizes, exported at scale 2.
- **Edge treatment:** soft vector edges, ink outline on silhouettes, thin crease lines inside volumes.
- **Layering:** soul fire and glows sit over everything they belong to; contact shadows and pools of light on the ground (`ctx.ground`, under the parts and the contour).
- **Motion:** loops close exactly (`t = frame / frames`); see the `diorama-volume` motion reference.

## Stage 7: effects, items and rarities, the HUD

The pieces of the game that are neither characters nor scenery (guide `docs/guides/volume/06-effects-items-and-hud.md`). The same palette, light (upper left) and line weights as the actors, and one rule above the rest: **soul green is the only saturated accent, and it marks only the legendary tier and what is active** (soul fire in an effect, a selected slot, the soul bar, a rune). Everything else is a muted metal, bone, stone or cloth; the one other hue is the blood of the health bar. Every size below is in logical px (world units for effects; exported at scale 2), and every colour is a theme token (`PALETTES`, `RAMPS`, `UI_PALETTES`, `UI_RAMPS`), never a hex.

### Effects (`src/vfx`, catalog `VFX`, one asset per entry in `sheets/vfx`)

An effect is light and colour: a saturated core (the `glow` role, `VOLUMES.soul`), a darker rim, a pale hot centre, drawn with `object.custom` at the effect's origin, with its floor marks (pools of light, rings) in `ctx.ground`, `height` below the origin. Timing: an anticipation frame or two, the peak early (`hit` on frame 1), a fall-off; loops close on `t = frame ÷ frames` (every motion is whole cycles of `t`); one-shots end on an empty frame. Random details come from `effectRng`, never `Math.random`. A projectile's origin sits on the socket that cues it, so the effect's `height` must match the socket's: a hand is 0.55 of the character (44), and a creature that spits from low down needs its own effect.

### Items and rarities (`src/items`, catalog `ITEMS`, one icon per item in `sheets/icons`)

- **Icon:** a square of **48** logical px (96 exported) with **5** px of padding: the drawn item is measured, then drawn again at the scale that fits the padded square, centred, so every icon fills its slot the same way. The ground is a dark slot with a soft glow of the tier's colour; the frame is an ink line, a band of the tier's colour with a bevel (lit top and left, dark bottom and right) and a thin dark line inside, and a small corner gem for epic and legendary.
- **Rarities** (`rarity.ts`; the tier lives in the frame and the ground only, the item keeps its own colours): the tiers are metals, and only the top one burns the accent.

- **Gear items** (`gear.ts`, 12): parts of the gear kit drawn as a character wears them with the body hidden, through `loot.ts`'s characters (the bodies parts rest on drawn dark and hollow, so a helm shows its inside and two soul eyes; the scythe held along a diagonal with its lantern hung in the clear). `facing` picks the view. `gear.hosts: 'hide'` and `spin` were compared and not kept for these (a hidden skull leaves a floating shell and loses the eyes; `spin` leaves the lantern small and the blade edge-on).
- **Pieces of their own** (`amulet.ts`, `armor.ts`, `cloth.ts`, `coin.ts`, `flask.ts`, `keys.ts`, `scroll.ts`, `shard.ts`, `tome.ts`, `worn.ts`; 19): built from `@core/volume` parts at a design size and fitted to the square, so only proportions show (a flask is 15 tall, a key 17 long, a coin 9 across: two to four times life size; a belt 11 across, a greave 16 long, a bracer 8 long, a shield a 16 across buckler). The ones that stand for a part of the gear kit (round shield, greave, bracer, leg wraps, belt, skull buckle, pouch, tunic, tabard, high collar, baldric) also carry `gear: { part }`: the piece draws the icon, the part says what it equips, and the atlas carries `gear` and, where the part has an equipment slot, `equip` (shield, greave, bracer, greave for the wraps, belt).
- **Pairs that could read alike differ in silhouette at 1x:** the greave stands upright (a tall plate, knee cop on top, tipped 62° and spun 16°) and the bracer is a short flared cuff lying with its open mouth to the viewer; the belt is a coiled ring with an iron frame buckle and the skull belt a straight strap with a pointed tail and a steel plate carrying a bone skull; the high collar is an open ring of crimson with two raised tips, brass hooks at the front and a dark trim (worn, the part is a plate behind a neck, and on its own it read as a bowl on two legs).
